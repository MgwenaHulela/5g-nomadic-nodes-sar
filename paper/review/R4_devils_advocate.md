## Strongest Counter-Argument

The paper's thesis, that one should bound queues and check liveness before learning where to fly, rests on two failures that look like defects of the OpenAirInterface UE, not properties of UAV-borne 5G cells. The authors write that "In OAI, a radio-link failure terminates the UE process" and that the backlog "was accepted and held by the OAI UE". They also concede that "a commercial modem may bound or drop uplink data." Remove those two implementation artefacts and the headline "largest gain" goes away. What remains is a learning layer that, by the paper's own account, forecasts weakly (worst held-out ROC-AUC 0.538) and places no better than hover.

The comparison behind "the largest gain came not from learning" is also unbalanced. The cap is measured against a broken baseline: uncapped, with minutes of queueing. The learning layer is measured against strong baselines (hover and centroid) after the cap has already repaired the system. It is also trained on pre-cap data whose "forest labels are partly queue backlog". So the learner was trained on the pathology the cap removes and then deployed where that pathology is gone. Its failure in closed loop fits training–deployment shift at least as well as any limit of terrain-blind learning. The ordering lesson in the title is partly built into the experiment rather than found by it.

The closed-loop numbers also cannot carry the abstract's headline loss. Each arm is one 90-min run on a different night. The paper's own same-policy null control shows night-to-night swings of +18.7 and −21.7 points. The reported exploring-learner losses of 18–27 points fall inside that envelope, and within-run bootstraps "do not capture run-to-run variation."

On this reading, what the paper shows is that an emulated OAI stack needs a rate limiter and a watchdog. That is a useful engineering note for testbed builders, but it does not establish a management principle for aerial cells.

## Ignored Alternative Explanations/Paths

1. **Emulator compute bound rather than radio.** The drain rates of 0.13–0.49 Mb/s per forest link, and possibly the size of the backlog, could reflect how fast the OAI rfsim/UE processes run on a shared host rather than radio capacity under 24–30 dB attenuation. Host CPU and real-time load during the static test is not reported. If drain is compute-bound, "weak NLoS uplinks" is not the right causal story.
2. **Training–deployment shift as the explanation for C4.** A retrained, cap-aware learner is the obvious control, and the conclusion lists it as future work. Without it, "learning does not help" and "learning trained on a broken system does not help" cannot be told apart.
3. **Feedback through the safety guards.** Robots react to link loss, so uncapped runs may produce worse patrol geometry (halting or lingering in bad spots). Part of the cap's gain may come from changed robot behaviour rather than from queueing alone.
4. **Simpler fixes give the same C2 result.** The fixed 100 kb/s cap already removes all C2 loss. The adaptive part matters only for video goodput, which in forest is about the same as uncapped (0.10–0.42 vs 0.13–0.49 Mb/s). Standard delay-based background congestion control such as LEDBAT (RFC 6817), or an adaptive-bitrate encoder, is the natural baseline for "delay-adaptive" and is not compared.
5. **The smooth channel limits learnability.** The appendix notes that the AWGN channel with large-scale loss only "may itself limit what a short-horizon forecaster can learn". Weak forecasting may come from the channel model, not from terrain-blindness, so the negative learning results may say more about the testbed than about the method.
6. **Over-censoring by the dead-process rule** (M7). If true outages with frozen counters are censored, the remaining forecasting task is easier or harder in ways the paper does not characterise.

## Missing Stakeholder Perspectives

- **SAR operators and teleoperators:** whether 0.10–0.42 Mb/s video at about 1 s latency is good enough for victim detection or teleoperation. Video is often the mission payload, and the paper's figure of merit is C2 only.
- **Hardware and modem vendors:** whether commercial UEs or modems buffer and fail like the OAI UE. The paper's central lesson depends on this, and no vendor documentation or measurement is cited.
- **UAV flight operations:** the energy and endurance cost of altitude probing and the airspace constraints. A learner that climbs and probes uses battery that hover does not.
- **Network operators running such cells:** whether a robot-side cap is deployable and who configures it. It needs software on every robot, which conflicts with the "no change to the RAN" deployability argument when robots come from several vendors.

## Unexamined Premise

The paper assumes that problems found in an emulated OAI stack are problems of "the managed system" in general. The framing "silent failure of the managed system" treats an implementation artefact as a system property. The whole "before learning" ordering depends on that premise, and it is not argued for.

## Observations (Non-Defects)

- The authors state negative results plainly ("C1 holds; C2 and C4 hold weakly; C3 holds as a negative result"). This is a real strength and will help the paper against reviewers who distrust positive-only ML-for-networking papers.
- The same-policy null control (Table A3), and the decision to mark urban and hills uninterpretable, is unusually honest and methodologically sound. Applying it consistently (M9, C1 above) would make it stronger.
- The forbidden-feature guard, which excludes injected path loss and the received power algebraically equivalent to it, closes an obvious leakage route that many emulation papers leave open.
- The static load test (Table I, Fig. 2), with its no-load phases before and after video and its fixed versus adaptive cap arms, is a clean mechanistic experiment. It is the most convincing evidence in the paper.
- The paper keeps its comparisons with the earlier SATNAC study separate ("numbers are not comparable across the two papers") and does not claim that the queueing mechanism explains the earlier forest result.
